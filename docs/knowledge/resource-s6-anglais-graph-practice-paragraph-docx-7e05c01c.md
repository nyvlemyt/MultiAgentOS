# S6 - Anglais — Graph Practice PARAGRAPH.docx

## Problem

Write a structured academic paragraph in English that accurately describes the trends shown in a line graph over time.

## Solution

Follow a three-part model paragraph: (1) Introductory sentence naming the graph subject, time frame, and variables ('The graph illustrates…'); (2) Body sentences describing each data series using precise trend vocabulary — peaks, falls, rises, plateaus; (3) Closing interpretive sentence linking converging trends to a possible explanation. Use the vocabulary bank below to signal direction and intensity:

| Movement | Vocabulary |
|---|---|
| Rise slowly | rose gradually |
| Rise sharply | saw a sharp rise |
| Reach maximum | reached a peak |
| Stay flat | remained stable |
| Fall slowly | fell / declined |
| Fall sharply | significantly decreased / plummeted |
| Reach minimum | bottomed out |

Example application (Australia fast-food 1975–2000): Pizza started lowest, rose steadily, peaked in 1995, then stabilised. Fish & Chips began highest, fell to 1980, recovered to 1985, then plummeted to below 38 portions/year by 2000. Hamburgers mirrored Pizza's sharp rise until ~1995, then levelled off. The closing sentence attributes the 1995 plateau of both Pizza and Hamburgers to a possible new competitor or growing health awareness.

## Variations

- For bar charts: replace time-series verbs with comparison language ('X is significantly higher than Y in…').
- For pie charts: use proportion language ('accounts for / represents X% of…').
- Fill-in-the-blank practice: blanks in the model paragraph target the 1995 plateau verb ('level off / stabilise'), the shared 20-year upward trajectory, and the contrastive fate of Fish & Chips ('continue to decline / plummet').

## Pitfalls

- Omitting the time frame or variable names in the opening sentence makes the paragraph unanchored.
- Using only one or two vocabulary words throughout (repeating 'increased' / 'decreased') loses marks for lexical range.
- Forgetting to contrast trends between series — the paragraph must show relationships (Conversely / However / Interestingly).
- Confusing 'peaked' (highest point) with 'stabilised' (flat after rise) — these are sequential, not synonymous.
- Skipping the interpretive closing sentence leaves the paragraph purely descriptive and weaker academically.
