# Anglais — Cyber Hacking EFREI.pdf

## Summary

Anglais EFREI course unit on cyber hacking: definitions, vocabulary (malicious/ethical hackers, hacktivists, BYOD, social engineering, water-holing, digital footprint, ransomware, whaling), and a case study on the July 2020 Twitter hack involving 130 high-profile accounts and a $118,000 bitcoin scam.

## Fields/API

**name**: Definition — Hacking
**value**: Use of unconventional or illicit means to gain unauthorized access to a digital device, computer system, or computer network.
**name**: Vocabulary Set 1 — Hacker types
**value**: Malicious Hackers (criminal intent), Ethical Hackers (authorized security testing), Hacktivists (politically/socially motivated hacking).
**name**: Vocabulary Set 2 — Business terms
**value**: BYOD (Bring Your Own Device), Social Engineering (manipulating people to divulge info), Water-Holing (compromising a website frequented by a target group), Digital Footprint (data trail left online), Ransomware (malware that encrypts data for ransom), Whaling (phishing targeting senior executives).
**name**: Legal term
**value**: Felony: a serious crime. Relevant charges in the case study include fraudulent use of personal information.
**name**: Case Study — Twitter Hack (July 15, 2020)
**value**: 130 high-profile accounts breached (Obama, Biden, Musk, Bezos, Apple, Uber). Perpetrators compromised Twitter's internal admin tools. Posted fake tweets offering to double bitcoin sent to attacker wallets. 415 transfers made, ~$118,000 collected.
**name**: Suspects arrested
**value**: Three people: a 17-year-old from Florida (alleged mastermind, 30 felony charges including 10 counts of fraudulent use of personal information), a 22-year-old American, and a 19-year-old from the UK.
**name**: Key vocabulary definitions (glossary)
**value**: alleged (e) / involvement (a) / candidate (d) / breached (c) / scam (b) / perpetrator (g) / legitimate (f) / suspect (i) / resident (n) / attorney (l) / felony (m) / fraudulent (j) / anonymously (h) / consequence (k).
**name**: True/False answers
**value**: 1-T, 2-T, 3-T, 4-T, 5-F (not all teenagers), 6-T, 7-F (30 charges against the 17-year-old, not one 'man'), 8-F (~$118,000, not over a million).
**name**: Speaking activity
**value**: Role-play as Twitter Cyber-Security Officers: present employee data-safety instructions and demonstrate an antivirus/secured network/cloud tool. Prep time: 20 minutes, done in pairs.

## Constraints

- Course material is English-language instruction for EFREI students; vocabulary is cybersecurity + legal register.
- Case study is factual (July 2020 Twitter hack) but presented as a reading comprehension exercise, not a forensic report.
- Speaking activity requires live pair work and a tool demonstration — not reproducible as a static document.

## Examples

- Social Engineering: an attacker calls an employee pretending to be IT support and tricks them into revealing their password.
- Whaling: a CEO receives a spoofed email from the 'CFO' requesting an urgent wire transfer.
- Water-Holing: attackers compromise a niche industry forum known to be visited by employees of the target company.
