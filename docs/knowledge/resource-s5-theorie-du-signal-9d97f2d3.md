# S5 - Théorie du signal

## Summary

Course materials for S5 Signal Theory, covering the mathematical foundations of signal analysis: complex numbers, signal classification and measurement, Fourier series development, Fourier transform, and convolution product. Includes lecture chapters, a written production, and 13 problem sets (Q01–Q13).

## Fields/API

**name**: Chap 0 — Nombres complexes
**description**: Foundations of complex numbers required for signal representation in the frequency domain.
**name**: Chap 1 — Classification et mesures
**description**: Taxonomy of signals (deterministic/random, periodic/aperiodic, energy/power) and associated measurement metrics.
**name**: Chap 2 — Développement en série de Fourier
**description**: Fourier series expansion of periodic signals: coefficients, convergence conditions, and spectral representation.
**name**: Chap 3 — Le produit de convolution
**description**: Convolution product: definition, properties, and role in LTI system analysis.
**name**: Chap 4 — La transformation de Fourier
**description**: Fourier transform for aperiodic signals: definition, properties (linearity, time-shift, duality, Parseval), and inverse transform.
**name**: Production écrite
**description**: Written academic production by Melvyn Saad synthesising course concepts.
**name**: Q01–Q13 (problem sets)
**description**: Thirteen graded exercise sheets covering the full course syllabus.
**name**: Théorie du signal.pdf
**description**: Main course reference document consolidating the full signal theory syllabus.

## Constraints

- Materials are from an S5 (semester 5) engineering curriculum — assumes prior knowledge of calculus and linear algebra.
- Chap 3 (convolution) is numbered out of sequence relative to Chap 4 (Fourier transform) in the file list; the logical reading order is Chap 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4.
- Problem sets Q01–Q13 are provided as separate PDF files without answer keys listed.

## Examples

- Fourier series: decompose a square wave into its harmonic components using Chap 2 coefficients.
- Apply the convolution theorem (Chap 3 + Chap 4) to compute the output of an LTI filter in the frequency domain.
- Use complex number polar form (Chap 0) to express phasors and spectral coefficients.
