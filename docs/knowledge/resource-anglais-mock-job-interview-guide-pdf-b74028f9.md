# Anglais — Mock Job Interview Guide.pdf

## Problem

Students in the Job Market Skills course (Efrei Languages Dept.) must prepare and perform a mock job interview in English — covering job-ad analysis, question design, professional delivery, and peer feedback — as a graded oral presentation (20 pts).

## Solution

1. **Find a job ad** relevant to your field (full-time, internship, apprenticeship, etc.) and share it with your partner at least one week before the presentation date.
2. **Design interview questions** based on the partner's job ad: prepare at least 6 standard questions + at least 1 behavioral question per role; tailor every question to the job requirements.
3. **Prepare answers** using the S.T.A.R. method for behavioral questions (Situation → Task → Action → Result). Use bullet-point notes only — no full script.
4. **Dress professionally** and rehearse aloud, ideally on camera, to self-evaluate intonation, eye contact, and body language.
5. **Follow the three-part interview structure**:
   - *Greetings/Introduction*: interviewer welcomes interviewee, small talk, brief company/role description.
   - *Information Exchange*: self-presentation, skills/experience questions (60% interviewee / 40% interviewer speaking time); include follow-up questions and authentic reactions.
   - *Wrap-Up*: interviewee restates fit/motivation; interviewer signals end and MUST invite questions; interviewee asks 2–3 specific researched questions; both confirm next steps; professional goodbye.
6. **Manage time**: Option A = max 10 min interview + 5 min feedback (15 min total). Option B = 7 min per role, both students play interviewer and interviewee (14 min total).
7. **Audience participation**: non-presenting students give structured feedback (slips, forms, discussion); participation counts toward their own grade.

## Variations

**Option A** — roles assigned randomly on the day; one student interviews, the other is interviewed; 10 min + 5 min class feedback.
**Option B** — both students play both roles sequentially; 7 min per role; overprepare since nerves accelerate pace.
**Behavioral questions** — use S.T.A.R. structure to answer (see Additional Resources for the leadership example with all four elements: VP role context → 25% attendance goal → survey + focus group actions → 150% attendance result).
**AI use** — permitted for practice only; generating a memorisable script = plagiarism (0/20 + disciplinary council).

## Pitfalls

1. Complaining about past employers or colleagues.
2. Being underprepared: too many hesitations, unfamiliarity with own examples.
3. Showing no enthusiasm or interest in the role.
4. Ignoring body language and eye contact.
5. Long answers to simple questions, short answers to complex ones.
6. Failing to connect personal skills to the specific job requirements.
7. Being vague — no names, numbers, or concrete examples.
8. Oversharing personal life details.
9. Not asking any questions about the company or role.
10. Asking what the company offers rather than what you bring.
11. Abrupt ending with no follow-up questions or professional close.
12. Exceeding the time limit → instructor stops you and deducts points.
13. Using a full script or AI-generated text → automatic 0/20.
