# The Problem

## Thesis

Expiration dates are unreliable estimates, causing massive food waste; FridgeGuard is a battery-free chemical smart label that provides real-time color-coded freshness detection to replace guesswork.

## Context

1.3 billion tons of food are wasted annually, costing retailers over $400B/year and accounting for 10% of global greenhouse gas emissions. Both consumers and retailers discard food too early because printed use-by dates do not reflect actual spoilage. The global food packaging market exceeds $300B, creating a large addressable market for freshness-sensing solutions.

## Reasoning

FridgeGuard addresses the root cause — imprecise date-stamping — by embedding a chemical sensor directly into a label. The color signal (green/yellow/red) requires no power or electronics, keeping unit cost at $0.10 with a $0.30 sale price. The B2B2C distribution model (sold to producers and retailers, used by consumers) aligns incentives across the supply chain. A patented sensor and absence of low-cost accurate competitors form the defensible moat. Seed funding of €300,000 targets final prototype, industrial pilot, and production launch.

## Trade-offs

The solution is simple and low-cost but depends on chemical sensor accuracy across diverse food categories and storage conditions. Battery-free design limits digital data capture in the short term, though a roadmap toward data analytics is mentioned. Market penetration requires convincing producers and retailers to change labeling workflows. Regulatory approval for food-contact chemical labels may vary by region and add time-to-market risk.
